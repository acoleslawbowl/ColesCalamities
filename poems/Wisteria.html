Wisteria Tinted Sunglasses

Cole Stanton

August 12th, 2026

Your vision is filled with paint strokes
The sun paints ribbons in the air
Through leaves of orchid trees
It becomes glitter on the grass
And past the shadow of the orchid leaves
The paint colors the field both orange and green
Before it greys under a mindless cloud
Around you are the songs of countless swallows
They sing your praise
Past a garden of tender tulip flowerbeds and trimmed hedges
Vines coupled with vibrant wisteria adorn the intricate walls
French white accents, no thoughtless meander
Weaving around no ordinary color

September, do you think of it?
A fountain splashes beside you
You had done the laundry
Watched the unmistakably warm and orange sun rise over a city
Your mind was running, sprinting
A million different emotions
Bounce off walls and collide with one another
You were no more than a heap of excited atoms
You gladly welcomed this

At the other end of two diamonds
A person so familiar your heart aches
If you twist,
Angle a gem precisely
Two light lines converge
And so it is.
It is awkward
But it is seamless
All worry melted
When you two built a rose for the garden

Your small insignificant brain
Will never truly matter
And yet, drunk on love and cured of its ails
It paints grand landscapes
Enamored trinkets of the smallest detail
It felt like nothing was as significant
A light cast through wisteria tinted glass
Transforms ordinary walks, smells, sights
Into something that seemed so bright
As if the day melts into long colors of sunrise
But the car rolled to a stop
You took one last gaze
Before they went away

Oh, how drunk were you
Not even a day after
A migraine ensued
Yet, the colors never faded
Your regular days took you on walks
Right past where you two
Rebelled against the world
You were delirious with hope, excitement
A tender pull of your heartstrings which you gladly proclaimed
It pulled the paintbrush across the canvas in your mind
A thousand different ways,
This flower reflected
Amethyst, Jam, Lavender, Lilac, Violet
You never thought to ask if they reciprocate,
If they ever felt the same

Have a good life.
The fragile glass shatters
Of course, you would have taken a migraine
Over a pain so blinding
A stabbing, a lacerating of your chest
Something no more real than your intoxication
But the glass shards still cut you
And you would’ve preferred it real
And that night
The cafeteria worker could have never known
What just shifted your paradigm
What turned your kaleidoscope monochrome

With no more sunlight from its purple star
The wisteria withered away on the castle walls
The painfully vivid memories
You walked past each day
Don’t seem to fade
The castle becomes dull
The wind freezes
The clouds still
The grass is hard, brittle
You don’t think of the flowers
Some might say,
Maybe it’s just winter
But you know that it would’ve been more vibrant that way

Yet it is not long before the color
Seeps back into the painting
Watercolor first finds the orchids
And then spreads outwards
You watch as crocuses grow into the tulip fields
The winds push the clouds once again
A family of sparrows finds a home
In the vines that once held your beloved delirium
Maybe the glasses you wear are still tinted lavender
Maybe there are still a few trickles of wisteria
